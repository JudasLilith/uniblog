I want to share the time when I used a condom for a 400$ project at a international hackathon.
It might sound weird, I know, but I'm sure I can give you a plentiful explanation.

This summer, I was invited to go to a international hardware hackathon: Fallout. It was held on Shenzhen, China; the electronics capital of the world. I've been working on projects to go to this competition, and I was feeling a wave of fufillment and accomplishments over the nights I spent working on my project. When we first got to our venue, I met people from all over the world: from India, Finland, Deutschland, Russia, Slovakia and so much more. What's made me even more excited is that these participants were here for the same reason I was; they loved to make hardware projects, and shared the same interests as me. When everyone was rounded up, we were shown the theme of the competition: The world is ending in 7 days, and we need to create our last project before the apocalypse. We first got split into groups of three for us to come up with ideas, and all of us had different projects they wanted to make: one wanted a face-recognition turret, one wanted an electric wheelchair, and i wanted to make a taser cage. After our discussions and watching as the other teams declare their projects, I decided to encorporate every aspect of each member of the team into one thing we can agree on: an Ultimate vehicle for trnasportation in the apocalypse, a motorized wheelchair with an automated crossbow turret, and a barrier made of high voltage tasers to fend off anyone approaching the unit. We had access to Hua Xiang bei, the world's largest electronics market, and we stocked up on various electronic parts for a great deal. We thought we had gotten everything, but there was one problem. There was nothing to make the crossbow out of. We thought we could have used the mall right next to our venue, and we left that to the very last step of our project, but the supposedly large mall didn't have a place for us to buy fitness bands. We were trying to think of different ways, like tying up hairbands together to a longer piece, or lightening the load of the turret arrows so that we could use a weaker, office rubber band. Both of these ideas were improbable, as the hairbands lacked the tension and the elasticity, while the turret arrows were going to take more than 4 hours to print again with the 3D printer. It all seemed hopeless, as our project would've been no different than a giant rolling face recognizer than an actual passion project capable of shooting people, but until I realized something - we needed something elastic, long, and durable, and we need it pronto; why not a condom? 
I was very embarrassed to go to buy the condom, however. It would plant the old lady working at the store a very impactful scene if 3 young men go into her shop, browse only condoms, then leave with a pack of one. I contemplated this decision so many times, but I was able to muster up the courage to go to the convenience store. I went inside, picked the box, then paid for the product all without making eye contact with the lady. just for that time, I felt like I murdered someone. However, as time passed bit by bit, I was getting more excited about the fact that our project could finally become complete, and I was getting less and less embarrassed over my purchase. I was able tie the condoms up into a tight and sturdy link, which saved our turret. From this experience, I learned that there is nothing to be embarrassed about when following your goals. There is nothing wrong with trying to solve a problem, no matter how socially taboo it can be.
